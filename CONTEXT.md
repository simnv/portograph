# portograph — Domain Context

Glossary and invariants for the project. Use these terms exactly; do not drift to synonyms. When a
concept you need is missing here, that is a signal — either you are inventing language the project
does not use, or there is a real gap to record.

`portograph` turns a YAML description of nodes, ports, links and groups into a laid-out diagram
(SVG), choosing the drawing technique per part of the diagram so the result reads like an engineer
drew it. Network diagrams first; software architecture and electrical panels next.

---

## Core vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Not to be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| **node** | A drawn box with a label, a class, and zero or more ports. The unit that groups contain and links connect *through*. | *device* (network profile alias), *component* (architecture alias) |
| **port** | A named or anonymous attachment point on a node's **side**. Every link end attaches to a port; a link never attaches to a node directly. Named ports render (the pill); anonymous ports do not. A port may carry a **class**: a profile *role* (`uplink`, `peer`, …) paints the pill and earns a legend row; a *status* (`up`, `down`, …) paints its stroke (`admin-down` also greys fill and text) or fades it. | *interface* (network alias), *terminal* (electrical alias) |
| **side** | One of `top`, `right`, `bottom`, `left`. Each port has exactly one side, assigned by the engine from the peer's position unless the author fixes it. | |
| **link** | A connection between two ports. Has a **class** (which decides its *family*) and optionally a **route** override. | *edge* (graph-theory word; avoid), *connection* |
| **group** | A labelled container holding nodes. A node belongs to exactly one group. A group has a **tier**, is a **flank**, or has a parent group, and has a **shape**. | *location* (d3-fd's word), *zone*, *area* |
| **tier** | An integer ordering rank on a group giving its row in the top-to-bottom hierarchy. Tier 0 is the top. Inferred from node classes by the profile when omitted; an explicit `tier:` always wins over class inference. Effective tier bands are compact after normalisation while preserving explicit-rank order. A document tier expands into one or more **sub-tiers** in stage 1; every stage after it reads sub-tiers. | *layer* (reserved for OSI/link-class layers) |
| **class rank** | An integer the profile's tier-inference table gives a node class, split in the middle band by `MIDDLE_CLASS_ROWS` (router, switch, server). A class that no row of the table names has none. | a **rank** (a position in one group) |
| **anchor** | Of a group: a member with a routed link to the tier above, or, when no member has one, a member with a routed link to the tier below. Anchors take rank 0, or the last rank when they link to the tier below. A group with no anchor has one rank. | *anchor tier*; a link's *anchor* point |
| **peer link** | A routed link inside a group whose two ends need not be ranked apart: both class ranks are defined and equal, or both ports are fixed to `left` and `right`. It never lengthens a rank path. | the `peer` port role |
| **rank** | An integer ordering of a group's members from the top of the group, derived from the routed links inside the group. Rank 0 holds the members that link to the tier above. A group with no anchor has one rank. | *tier* (orders groups, not members) |
| **rank cell** | The layout group that holds the members of one rank of one document group (`G#r`). A cell is a `row` group to every stage. It has its own sub-tier, no label, no padding of its own (the frame margin is held in the first and last cell; edges between cells hold 0) and no frame. | *frame* |
| **sub-tier** | One entry of the dense tier list the layout runs on. Document tier `t` expands to `R(t)` sub-tiers, where `R(t)` is the largest rank count among the groups of tier `t`. Every stage that reads `group.tier` reads a sub-tier. | *tier* (the document's own ordering) |
| **frame** | The drawn box of a document group, derived after placement the way tier bands are: the outermost body of its cells plus 20 px per side, plus the label band, held in the first and last cell. A **child frame** is drawn inside its parent's frame. The frame is the ADR-0013 obstacle and the ADR-0011 title owner. A foreign link's lane stands outside the frame, at one x for all its sub-tiers. A link that ends inside the frame may transit it, and the frame grows to hold its lane. | *rank cell* |
| **column** | A frame's box plus 20 px slack on each side. The wall centring and the overlay sweeps read. Siblings of one tier are disjoint columns a band gap apart, packed anchor tier first, with each axis on the mean x of its routed feeds. Centring spends the slack, and never leaves less than a band gap between frame boxes. | *grid* column (a corridor inside one `grid` group) |
| **ranked** | The default group shape. A ranked group with one rank is a `row` in every respect. | `grid` (explicit) |
| **flank** | A group placed *beside* a tier rather than in it: `align: left` or `align: right`. Its links to the tier are lateral. A flank has a **primary peer** — the tier node it shares the most links with. | a tier member with `position` hints |
| **shape** (of a group) | How the group arranges its nodes: `ranked` (default), `row`, `bar`, `grid`, `ring`, `free`. M1 ships `ranked` (default), `row`, `bar` and `grid`. | *layout algorithm* |
| **grid** | An explicit shape; `ranked` is the default. A group shape that packs its nodes into shared columns and stacked rows. **A column is a corridor** — a node's downward link drops through whatever its column holds below it — so a column holds at most one node that exits downward and at most one that exits upward, and the column count is bounded below by the larger of those two counts — by the node count where any node exits **both** ways, since that node needs the first row and the last at once. Within that bound it is the count whose **stage-4** box comes closest to square, ties to the fewer columns, measured on the ranked arrangement the grid will really pack; stage 5 re-lays the same count out at final sizes, so the published box is near square and not square. A group whose nodes all exit one way, or that holds one node exiting both ways, therefore takes one row, which is the `row` shape. Rows stand a **band gap** apart, because a bottom port above the last row leaves the group past every row below it. A node whose links leave upward takes the first row and one whose links leave downward the last; a flank's primary peer takes the outer end of its own row. One grid group anywhere re-packs every tier and re-stacks every band, so the shape cannot be adopted group by group without re-reading the sheet. A group that holds both ends of a **bundle** does not take the shape at all — rows are stacked before the rules that grow a body run, so no arrangement can promise that bundle its channel — and such a group is a `row` to every rule, not only to the packer. Where an arrangement *can* answer, it does rather than refusing: a count that would put in **one column** two nodes joined by a routed link whose two ports the author fixed to `top` is not a candidate, because nodes of one column overlap in x, so the lower one's `top` stem points into the upper one's body and the upper one's port is on the far side of it, above the first row where only the group's own padding lies. Different **rows** are not the fault, different **columns** are not a fault, and `bottom`/`bottom` is measured to publish in the arrangement `top`/`top` fails in — below the last row lie the group padding and the tier gap, and above the first row there is neither. | a portrait `row` |
| **bar** | A group shape where each node renders as a horizontal bar spanning its attached ports' x-range; links onto a bar are vertical drops, ports ordered by source x. Bars may stack; a drop may cross an upper bar to reach a lower one. | a wide `row` node |
| **region** | The space between two adjacent tiers, or between a flank and its tier. Routing strategy is chosen per region by what is on each side. | *group* |
| **lane** | A reserved vertical corridor (`laneW` = pill width + gap ≈ 34 px), in the margin beside a group, for one link transiting a tier it does not terminate in. A lane has an **owner**; it is an obstacle to every link, its owner included. The owner passes through it only as the collinear run the lane reserved, never as a routed gap. Reserved before anything is pinned. | *channel*; the 16 px **member gap** inside a bundle |
| **anchor tier** | The tier with the most main-tier nodes, including bars and excluding flank nodes (ties: lowest effective tier index). Groups below it are centred under their feeds; groups above it are centred over their children. | |
| **junction** | A branch point in a tree of shared runs at one port. Source and target incidence use the same outward tree. Only declared shared runs are exempt from the overlap gate. It is model geometry reserved during placement, never a runtime `JunctionRef`: stage 8 stitches the reserved run back unchanged and routes only the gap beyond its terminal. | *bundle* |
| **band** | Reserved horizontal space: a **tier band** (the vertical extent a tier occupies) or a **label band** (space inside a group's top padding reserved for its label). | |
| **multiplicity label** | The measured count drawn beside one counted merge trunk, owned by the trunk's bundle. Stage 9 places exactly one per `mergeRuns` record; it clears every routed member segment and is never shortened. | a **link label** (one authored label, owned by its link, exempt only from its own supporting segment) |
| **supporting segment** | The one path segment a visible link label was finally measured against, persisted on the label. Its clearance zone is the only thing G3 exempts: text across the segment itself is still a hit, and a stale or absent reference exempts nothing. | the link's other segments |
| **skeleton** | The tier/group placement produced by ELK before region rules run. Lateral (flank↔tier) links are **withheld** from the skeleton so flanks land beside their tier. | *layout* (the whole result) |
| **bundle** | Two or more routed links between the same pair of nodes, routed through a channel and fanned to ports. The **channel** is the space between the two endpoint **bodies**, measured along the bundle's own normal; it exists only where the two bodies stand beside each other across that normal, in that order, and nothing stands between them — a node beside the channel but not in it is no obstacle, so adjacency is not the predicate. A group that holds **both** ends of a bundle therefore keeps its nodes on one line: it declines any shape that stacks them, takes `row` to every rule, and says which bundle asked (`GROUP_SHAPE_DECLINED`). The refusal is incomplete by construction — a bundle between a **flank** and one node of a stacked group has no group holding both ends, and stacks its reserved blocks across the rows regardless. Its **envelope** reserves the swept geometry, including stroke width. Parallel members cross once for each strict endpoint-order inversion; shared-port ties count as zero. A heavily inverted bundle draws **straight members** (stem, one diagonal, stem) instead of the orthogonal staircase, when the existing geometry holds the member gap between every pair of member segments G6 measures (ADR-0017). Engine order minimizes avoidable inversions where incidence and reservations permit it. Selected `route: merge` members share counted trunks; other members remain parallel. | *overlay* |
| **overlay** | A link family drawn thin, translucent and *unrouted* on its own layer beneath routed links. Used for flow-class links (e.g. NTP sync). Exempt from clearance gates. Its path is not routed but **derived**, and the rule is **straight by default, orthogonal by exception, and the exception is a node in the way** (maintainer, 2026-09-16): **one straight segment from one port's outward face to the other's**, at whatever angle, and nothing else — no stub (*"no point in stub then"*, maintainer, 2026-09-16) — unless the segment **fails to leave a port outward** or enters a **node body or a foreign port pill**, and then it is the orthogonal path that leaves each face outward by the **overlay port clearance** and goes round the shapes it would otherwise cross. *Leaving outward* is the branch's own condition: at each end the segment's direction out of that end may not point back along the port's own outward normal. Level counts as leaving, so two ports level on one side ride both faces and keep the straight segment; a port facing **away** from its partner does not, and takes the orthogonal branch, because a line leaving a port inward runs back under that port's own pill and the mask would cut it away from its endpoint mark. Because that condition holds, a link's **own two pills** cannot block its own straight segment and are not tested — its node bodies are. Nothing else blocks it: not another overlay line, not a group box, a lane, a routed path or a label. Placement's job is to position nodes so that as many overlay links as possible take the straight branch. | *bundle*, the **stub** technique (a different thing) |
| **group-link** | One link drawn between two groups (each with ≥ 2 nodes) standing for the **complete** bipartite set of same-class links between their nodes, labelled with the count; membership is kept in the model. Near-complete sets stay overlay. It is drawn on its members' own **family** layer — a set of overlay members is an overlay group-link — because aggregation says how many lines stand for the set, never on which layer they are drawn. M1: warns, draws the members. | *bundle* |
| **stub** | A link drawn as a label on each end node instead of a line ("→ loc1ntp001"), used when a route would cross more than two tiers or detour more than 3× the direct distance. Both ends are marked. | hiding a link |
| **family** (of a link) | `routed` or `overlay`, decided by the link's class through the profile. Physical links are routed; flow links are overlay. | *route* (the per-link technique within a family) |
| **profile** | A TypeScript module plus optional YAML overlay that supplies vocabulary aliases, tier-inference table, conventions (direction, which classes are routed vs overlay), default theme, icon set, and validators. Ships: `network`, `architecture`, `electrical`. | *theme* (only the visual part) |
| **golden** | A reference diagram the renderer must reproduce to the maintainer's satisfaction. M1 golden: `docs/design/target-example.jpeg`. Others: a three-tier LAN, `superspine.yaml`, the Enterprise NTP sheet. | a snapshot test |
| **legend** | The generated key to the symbols and line styles a diagram really draws. It appears only when more than one symbol or more than one line style is present, or a port role beside any other variant, lists exactly the classes and port roles present, and is measured during stage 9 into reserved canvas space below the drawn diagram. It is model geometry, owned by `legend`, never authored and never scored as part of the diagram's bounding box. | a **group label**; an authored key |
| **gate** | A hard pass/fail check on layout output (overlap, through-node, label hit, port stem, clearance, G9 `frame-contains`: a frame holds its cells and child frames, frames of one tier stay apart, no foreign node body lies inside a frame). Any failed gate fails the diagram. | *score* |
| **score** | A weighted soft quality number in [0, 1] computed from crossings, bends, continuity, compactness, alignment, flow, aspect, uniformity. Guards regressions; never repairs. | *gate* |

## Invariants

1. **Links attach to ports, never to nodes.** A link written to a bare node gets an anonymous port.
2. **A node has exactly one group.** A group may have one parent group; a child group has no tier of its own and is a span of whole ranks inside its parent.
3. **Node size is derived from its content and its ports:** the larger of the measured label/description/icon bounds, the author's `size:` floor, and per-side port counts × the measured pitch of that side. Never shrunk to fit.
4. **Port topology freezes before paths:** identity, count, side and author `order:` are fixed after the two side phases. Engine-selected order may still change where bar and bundle rules require it, including compatible primary-bundle order during flank alignment. Bundle rules may grow endpoint pitch and capacity, repack rows, re-level blocks and reserve crossing space before paths. Fixed lane x and owners remain immutable. Placement may grow or move geometry only through its declared rules; it never shrinks a node below its current content or author floor. Final placement freezes after all region rules.
5. **Meaning before geometry, space before use:** which technique a link gets is decided from the document (stage 1); lanes, bands, bundle rows and bar spans are reserved during placement, not discovered during routing.
6. **Every fixed path is an obstacle** for everything routed after it. All paths are validated together at the end.
7. **Region rules are the router for regular tiered diagrams.** libavoid routes only what the rules do not claim.
8. **The tier contract survives at any size:** ELK partitioning is always attempted; on exception **or deadline**, one ELK run per tier band, stacked; bands are validated after either path (ADR-0006).
9. **Nothing is styled by value in YAML.** Nodes, links and groups carry `class`; a profile theme and an optional `css:` block do the rest.
10. **The layout JSON is a first-class output.** SVG is one renderer of it; `score` reads it directly.
